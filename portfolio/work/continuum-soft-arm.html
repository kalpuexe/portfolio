Continuum Soft Robot Arm

An arm that bends along its whole length instead of pivoting at joints.

Role
Independent research — ongoing
Backing
i2i Stage 2 selection, Eaton India Foundation with COEP Technological University
Approach
Tendon-driven, constant curvature
Status
Work in progress

The idea

A conventional robot arm is a chain of rigid links turning about discrete joints. That works well inside a cell built around it, and badly almost everywhere else — around obstacles, into confined spaces, or anywhere the arm has to conform to something rather than reach past it.

A continuum arm bends continuously along its entire length, closer to a trunk than a crane. There are no joints to collide with, which is exactly what makes it interesting for automation in spaces that were never designed with a robot in mind.

How I approached it

I built the mechanism before modelling it. The first prototype is a stack of laser-cut discs threaded onto a flexible central backbone, with tendons running through holes near the disc edges. Pulling one tendon shortens that side and the whole structure curves — the bend distributes across every segment instead of concentrating at a hinge.

Working physically first meant the awkward parts surfaced early: tendon friction through the discs, stopping the backbone twisting under load, and how quickly the curvature stops being uniform once the arm carries anything at all.

Where it stands

The current build wraps the disc stack in a compliant sleeve, which gives a smooth continuous curve rather than a visibly segmented one.

The project was selected for Stage 2 innovation funding under Ignited Innovators of India, run by COEP Technological University with the Eaton India Foundation — external validation from people with no stake in it beyond whether it works.

It is still open research rather than a finished product. The honest summary is that the mechanism works and the modelling is still catching up with it.
