SaveSmart

A budgeting app that rebuilds your plan after an emergency.

Role
Product and build — solo
Timeline
2025
Built with
React, Capacitor
Status
Working Android APK

The problem

Budgeting apps are very good at telling you that you overspent. Almost none of them do anything about it. The plan you set at the start of the month stays fixed while reality drifts away from it, and the app's role is reduced to keeping score.

The failure case that bothered me is specific: a single unplanned ₹5,000 expense quietly takes a savings goal down with it, because nothing in the system reallocates the rest of the month to absorb the hit.

How I approached it

I ran a competitor teardown across YNAB, Mint, Monarch, Walnut and INDmoney. All of them are strong at tracking and categorisation; none of them treat the savings goal as the thing to be defended.

That gap became the positioning: SaveSmart is not an expense tracker, it is goal protection. The distinction drove every subsequent product decision.

What I built

I designed and built the app rather than mocking it up:

  • React mobile app packaged for Android with Capacitor
  • A reprioritisation engine that reallocates discretionary “wants” spending after an emergency
  • Goal-first framing throughout the UI, so the savings target stays the primary object
  • Figma designs for the flows ahead of implementation

Where it landed

A working APK addressing the ₹5,000-emergency scenario end to end, aimed at India's outcome-based budgeting market — users who care whether they hit the goal, not whether they logged the coffee.
