Solar Panel Cleaning Robot

A cleaning robot costed for the rooftops that actually need one.

Role
CAD design and concept
Built with
SolidWorks, Fusion 360
Focus
Mechanism design, design for manufacture
Status
Complete CAD design

The problem

Dust accumulation measurably reduces solar panel output, and in dry, dusty regions it does so quickly. Commercial cleaning robots exist, but they are priced and sized for utility-scale installations. For a small rooftop array the economics never work out, so the panels simply stay dirty.

The design question was therefore a cost question: what is the least mechanism that still cleans reliably?

How I approached it

I designed around brush rollers spanning the panel width, carried on a four-wheel-drive chassis that travels across the array. The cleaning is deliberately water-minimal — water is the expensive part of panel cleaning in exactly the dry, dusty regions where soiling is worst.

The other decision was to make the robot cover its own running cost: it carries a solar panel on its back and charges from it, so there is no trailing cable to manage and no separate power budget to justify.

What the design covers

  • Dual brush rollers sized to the panel width for single-pass coverage
  • Four-wheel drive with position encoders for travel control across the array
  • An onboard solar panel for self-charging between cleaning runs
  • A chassis proportioned for small rooftop arrays rather than utility scale
  • Component selection biased throughout towards cost and availability

Where it landed

A complete CAD design and concept package. The next step is a physical prototype to test whether the brush-and-encoder approach holds up against real soiling rather than assumed soiling.
