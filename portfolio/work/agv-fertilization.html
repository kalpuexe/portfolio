Co-inventor & design lead · 2023–24

Automated Guided Vehicle for Crop Fertilization

A granted design patent, and a prototype that actually drives.

Role
Co-inventor and mechanical design lead
Timeline
2023–25, across two generations
Built with
SolidWorks, Arduino, laser-cut acrylic, welded steel
Status
Design Patent No. 436182-001 — granted

The problem

Broadcast fertiliser spreading applies nutrients across a whole field at a roughly uniform rate. It is fast, and it is wasteful: the farmer pays for fertiliser that lands where nothing needs it, and the excess is hard on the soil over successive seasons.

The opportunity was to move from spreading everywhere to placing fertiliser where plants actually are — without demanding equipment a smallholder could never justify.

How I approached it

The first concept was over-scoped, as first concepts usually are. I used MoSCoW prioritisation to cut it back to an MVP that could be built and demonstrated with the materials and budget we had: hold fertiliser, meter it accurately, place it either side of the vehicle, and drive.

Everything that was interesting but unproven — vision-based plant detection, autonomous navigation, variable-rate control — was deliberately pushed out of the first build.

What I built

The prototype is a welded steel chassis carrying:

  • A laser-cut acrylic hopper sized for a demonstration payload
  • A metering mechanism beneath the hopper to control discharge rate
  • Servo-actuated spreader arms placing fertiliser to either side of the vehicle
  • Arduino control for the drive and actuation, with the full assembly modelled in SolidWorks first

The second generation

The patented vehicle is the small three-wheeled machine — one driven axle, a caster at the front, and just enough structure to carry the hopper. It was built in my third year and it proved the dispensing idea worked.

It also made its own limits obvious. Three wheels on soft, uneven field soil is a stability problem before it is anything else, and the frame had no payload capacity beyond the hopper it was designed around.

So in my fourth year I kept going. The second generation moves to four wheels on independent sprung suspension, carries a far larger two-compartment bed, and adds a pair of arms with grippers on top — turning a single-purpose spreader into a platform that can carry and handle things as well as dispense them.

  • Four-wheel independent suspension in place of the three-wheel caster layout
  • A two-compartment cargo bed sized for real payload rather than a demonstration hopper
  • Twin articulated arms with grippers for pick-and-place
  • A wider, lower chassis for stability on uneven ground

Where it landed

The design was registered as Design Patent No. 436182-001 on 30 October 2024 and issued on 16 December 2024 by the Patent Office, Government of India, under class 15-03.

That is the part I value most: it is external validation of the design by an examiner with no stake in the outcome, rather than a self-assessment.

The design is registered jointly with Government College of Engineering Amravati, Aditya Shantanu Joshi and Dr. Rajesh M. Metkar.
